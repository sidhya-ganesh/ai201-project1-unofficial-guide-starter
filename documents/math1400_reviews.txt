Course: MATH 1400 - Calculus I
University: University of Pennsylvania
Source: Rate My Professors / PennCourseReview

Professor: Robert Ghrist
Rating: 4.8/5
Review 1: Ghrist is genuinely one of the most impressive professors at Penn. His lectures are polished like TED talks — he uses beautiful visuals and his enthusiasm for math is infectious. The course is harder than a typical Calc I but you come out actually understanding what calculus means, not just how to do it mechanically. Absolutely take this if you can.

Review 2: The homework is harder than the exams, which is unusual but good. Ghrist uses a lot of topology and visual intuition which is very different from high school calculus. His online lecture videos (from Coursera) are also available and are a great supplement if you miss a class or need to review.

Review 3: Grading is fair. Midterms are challenging but predictable if you've done the homework carefully. The final is a beast but curved. Ghrist responds to emails unusually quickly for a professor of his stature. One of Penn's true academic celebrities.

Review 4: If you're used to AP Calculus this course will surprise you — it's more conceptual and visual. Don't expect to coast. But if you engage with the material it's one of the most rewarding math courses at Penn.

Professor: Jerry Kazdan
Rating: 4.1/5
Review 1: Kazdan is a Penn legend. He's been teaching here for decades and knows the material inside and out. His lectures are old-school — blackboard only, no slides — but his explanations are crystal clear. He takes questions seriously and never dismisses confusion. The exams reflect exactly what he covers in class.

Review 2: Very traditional teaching style. If you learn well from lecture and practice problems, Kazdan is excellent. If you need multimedia or variety, you might struggle. His office hours are a highlight — he'll sit with you for as long as it takes.

Review 3: The curve in this course is very generous. Kazdan seems to genuinely want everyone to succeed. Homework is assigned but not collected — which some students find liberating and others find dangerous. Do the homework anyway. The exams will expose you if you don't.
