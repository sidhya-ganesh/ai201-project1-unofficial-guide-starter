Course: MGMT 1010 - Introduction to Management
University: University of Pennsylvania (Wharton)
Source: Rate My Professors / Reddit r/UPenn / Wharton student forums

Professor: Adam Grant
Rating: 4.9/5
Review 1: Adam Grant is one of the most well-known professors in the world and he lives up to the hype. His lectures at Wharton are energetic, evidence-based, and genuinely funny. He challenges conventional wisdom about work and organizations and backs everything up with research. If you can get into his course, do it no matter what.

Review 2: Grant's course covers organizational behavior and management through a social science lens. It's not your typical business class — it's more like applied psychology. His assignments involve real reflection on your own behavior and motivations. Grade breakdown is mostly case studies and a final project.

Review 3: He's famous for being an organizational psychologist and author (Give and Take, Originals, Think Again) but he doesn't just lecture from his books. The content goes deeper than the popular writing. Very high-value course even for non-Wharton students who can get in.

Review 4: Getting into his course is nearly impossible as a non-Wharton student. The waitlist is long. Show up to the first class anyway — he sometimes lets in waitlisted students if they come in person.

Professor: Nicolaj Siggelkow
Rating: 4.3/5
Review 1: Siggelkow uses the case method exclusively — no lectures in the traditional sense. You come to class having read the case and the discussion is entirely student-driven. This is uncomfortable at first but you learn to speak up or get left behind. His cold-calling is nerve-wracking but fair.

Review 2: The workload is reading-heavy. You need to genuinely prepare each case, not just skim it. He asks probing follow-up questions that expose whether you've actually thought it through. Grading is based on participation and case write-ups. Exams are rare.

Review 3: His feedback on write-ups is excellent. He tells you exactly what analytical move you missed and why it matters. One of the more demanding Wharton professors but students who take the course seriously report significant growth in their business thinking.
