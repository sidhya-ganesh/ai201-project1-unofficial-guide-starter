Course: ECON 0100 - Introduction to Micro Economics
University: University of Pennsylvania
Source: Rate My Professors / PennCourseReview

Professor: Rebecca Stein
Rating: 4.4/5
Review 1: Stein is an incredible lecturer. She explains concepts clearly and uses lots of real-world examples from current events. Her slides are detailed and posted before class, so you can annotate them during lecture. Exams are multiple choice and short answer — no essays. She curves generously and the median is usually around a B+.

Review 2: Best econ professor I've had at Penn. She makes micro feel intuitive rather than just mechanical. Office hours are very helpful and she doesn't make you feel rushed. The final exam is cumulative but she provides a detailed study guide. Highly recommend.

Review 3: Stein's exams are fair but you need to understand the graphs deeply, not just memorize them. Practice drawing supply/demand shifts from scratch. Her recitation TAs vary in quality — try to get into one with strong reviews from upperclassmen.

Review 4: She posts practice exams and goes over them in review sessions before each midterm. Attendance at review sessions is basically mandatory if you want to do well. The material in weeks 8-10 (game theory) is harder than the rest of the course — don't underestimate it.

Professor: Francis Diebold
Rating: 3.5/5
Review 1: Diebold is a brilliant economist but not a natural teacher at the intro level. His lectures assume more math background than the course officially requires. That said, his notes are incredibly detailed and the course website has tons of supplementary material. If you're willing to teach yourself from his resources, you'll learn a lot.

Review 2: The curve is very generous — even if midterms go badly you can recover. Exams are more quantitative than conceptual. Make sure you're comfortable with basic calculus before taking this course with Diebold. Some students switch sections after the first week.

Review 3: His lecture style is very fast-paced. He covers everything on the syllabus but sometimes skips intuition in favor of formalism. Go to office hours if you're confused — he's actually quite patient one-on-one even if lectures feel impersonal.
