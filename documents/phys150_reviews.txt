Course: PHYS 0150 - Principles of Physics I
University: University of Pennsylvania
Source: Rate My Professors / PennCourseReview

Professor: Arjun Yodh
Rating: 4.0/5
Review 1: Yodh is a distinguished researcher who brings a lot of intellectual depth to introductory physics. His lectures are well-organized but move quickly. The course covers classical mechanics through a calculus-based approach so make sure your math is solid before starting. Exams are problem-solving focused — you won't be able to get by on conceptual understanding alone.

Review 2: The weekly problem sets are the backbone of this course. They are difficult and time-consuming but if you work through them carefully you will be prepared for exams. Do not rely on just reading the solutions — work the problems yourself. Collaboration is encouraged but do your own write-ups.

Review 3: Yodh's office hours are underutilized. Most students only show up before exams but he's available weekly and will help you think through the problem sets. The physics help room (David Rittenhouse Lab) is also staffed daily and is the best resource for homework help.

Review 4: The lab component is graded separately and has its own TA. Labs are not optional even if they feel disconnected from lecture. Lab reports are graded on analysis quality, not just whether you got the right number. Show your error analysis properly.

Professor: Evelyn Thomson
Rating: 4.5/5
Review 1: Thomson is a particle physicist and one of the clearest physics lecturers at Penn. She uses great analogies and never skips the intuition behind a formula. Her exams are fair and her practice exams closely mirror the real thing. She's enthusiastic about teaching and it shows.

Review 2: If you're pre-med or pre-engineering and need physics, Thomson is the section to be in. Her expectations are high but clearly communicated. She posts detailed solutions to all practice problems so you always know exactly where you went wrong.

Review 3: She holds review sessions before each exam and they are incredibly useful — she goes through not just what will be on the exam but how to think about each type of problem. Attendance is not mandatory but anyone who skips and then complains about their grade deserves no sympathy.
