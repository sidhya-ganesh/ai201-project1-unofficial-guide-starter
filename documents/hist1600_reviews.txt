Course: HIST 1600 - American History Since 1865
University: University of Pennsylvania
Source: Rate My Professors / Reddit r/UPenn

Professor: Thomas Sugrue
Rating: 4.7/5
Review 1: Sugrue is one of the most respected historians in the country and teaching with him is a privilege. His lectures are basically performances — he speaks without notes for 75 minutes and tells American history as a coherent narrative. No slides, no PowerPoints, just his voice and the blackboard. Take notes fast.

Review 2: The readings are substantial — 80-100 pages per week — but they are well-chosen and actually interesting. Don't skip them because he will reference them in lecture and you'll be lost. Papers are graded on argument quality and use of evidence, not length. Be concise and analytical.

Review 3: His exams are essays written in class. You get the question in advance, which sounds easy, but he expects a sophisticated, well-structured argument, not just a summary of readings. Draft your essay before the exam and practice writing it from memory.

Review 4: Sugrue is genuinely interested in students' opinions and creates space for discussion even in a large lecture. Go to his office hours if you're interested in his research — he's generous with time for curious students. His book on Detroit is assigned reading and it's genuinely excellent.

Professor: Eve Troutt Powell
Rating: 4.2/5
Review 1: Powell specializes in Middle Eastern and African history but her American history course is excellent. She brings a global perspective to US history that most students haven't encountered before. Her lectures are engaging and she encourages debate.

Review 2: The paper assignments are demanding but she provides very detailed rubrics. Her feedback on drafts is thorough — take advantage of the draft submission option. Grading is tough but fair. She takes academic writing seriously and so should you.

Review 3: Powell is accessible and supportive in office hours. If you're struggling with an argument she'll help you find it rather than just criticizing what you have. One of Penn's better humanities professors for students who want to be challenged.
